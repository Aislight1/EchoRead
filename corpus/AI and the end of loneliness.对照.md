# 中英对照

**1.** I have a couple of questions for you—but they’re personal.  
> 我有几个问题想问你们——但它们比较私人。  

**2.** So I’m not going to ask for a show of hands.  
> 所以我不会请大家举手示意。  

**3.** First, right now, at this stage of your life, are you lonely?  
> 首先，就在此刻，在你人生的这一阶段，你感到孤独吗？  

**4.** And if not, have you ever been lonely?  
> 如果此刻没有，那你曾经孤独过吗？  

**5.** My favorite description of loneliness comes from Zoë Heller's book 'Notes on a Scandal.'  
> 我对孤独最钟爱的描述，出自佐伊·赫勒的小说《丑闻笔记》。  

**6.** And in it, the narrator, who is played in the movie by Judi Dench, distinguishes between passing loneliness and something deeper.  
> 叙述者——电影中由朱迪·丹奇饰演——  

**7.** So she observes that people think they know what it's like to be lonely.  
> 因此她指出，人们自以为了解孤独的滋味。  

**8.** You know, they had a bad breakup or a rough time in summer camp.  
> 比如，他们经历过糟糕的分手，或在夏令营里过得不愉快。  

**9.** But then she says this.  
> 但她紧接着这样说道。  

**10.** About the drip, drip of long-haul, no-end-in-sight solitude, they know nothing.  
> 至于那种旷日持久、遥遥无期的孤寂所发出的‘滴答、滴答’声，他们一无所知。  

**11.** They don't know what it is to construct an entire weekend around a visit to the launderette, or to sit in a darkened flat on Halloween night, because you can't bear to expose your bleak evening to a crowd of jeering trick-or-treaters.  
> 他们不懂如何围绕一次洗衣店之行，精心安排整整一个周末。  

**12.** And she concludes with this: 'I have sat on park benches and trains and school room chairs feeling the great store of unused, objectless love sitting in my belly like a stone until I was sure I would cry out and fall,  
> 无具体对象的爱，如巨石般沉在腹中，直至我确信自己将失声痛哭、颓然倒地，  

**13.** flailing to the ground.'  
> 徒劳挥舞着坠向地面。'  

**14.** I like this quote for a few reasons.  
> 我喜欢这段引文，原因有三。  

**15.** It talks about solitude, and solitude is a big source of loneliness, but it also talks about love.  
> 而独处正是孤独的一大根源，  

**16.** You could be with somebody, but if you're not loved, not needed, not respected, you might feel lonely.  
> 未被需要，未被尊重，  

**17.** And then there's understanding.  
> 再者，还有理解。  

**18.** Suppose you have something in your head that's important to you, That matters a lot to you, but you feel others don't get it, or don't care to get it, or aren't trying hard enough to get it.  
> 试想，你心中怀揣着一件对你而言至关重要的事，  

**19.** This too could cause loneliness.  
> 这种情况同样可能引发孤独感  

**20.** Loneliness ravages the body as well as the soul.  
> 孤独感不仅侵蚀灵魂，也摧残身体  

**21.** A report by the Surgeon General associates it with greater risk of cardiovascular disease, dementia, stroke and the like.  
> 美国卫生总监发布的一份报告指出，孤独感会显著增加患心血管疾病的风险  

**22.** It's the equivalent of smoking 15 cigarettes a day.  
> 其危害程度相当于每天吸15支香烟  

**23.** Studies in the United States and United Kingdom find that about one in five people describe themselves as often or always lonely.  
> 约五分之一的人自述经常  

**24.** And the most at-risk group is young adults.  
> 而孤独风险最高的人群是年轻人  

**25.** I'm here to talk about a possible cure for loneliness and what it might mean.  
> 我今天要探讨一种可能的孤独症解方，以及它所蕴含的意义  

**26.** And I think we could see loneliness as similar to boredom.  
> 我认为，我们可以将孤独感类比为无聊感  

**27.** I'm old enough to remember when people were bored all the time.  
> 我的年纪足以让我记得，人们曾终日百无聊赖  

**28.** Long train rides, long car rides, long plane rides.  
> 漫长的火车旅程、汽车旅程与飞机旅程  

**29.** Late at night when the TV stations switched off and you were all alone, and you had to read.  
> 当电视台停止播送节目时  

**30.** (Laughter) But now most of us carry around our phones and those phones offer podcasts and movies and books and music and social media and so on.  
> （笑声）但如今，我们大多数人随身携带着手机  

**31.** And so for many of us, boredom is a thing of the past.  
> 因此，对许多人而言，无聊已成为过去式  

**32.** Well the cure for loneliness may also be found in our phones and our laptops.  
> 那么，孤独的解方或许同样蕴藏于我们的手机与笔记本电脑之中  

**33.** And it is, of course, generative AI.  
> 而这一解方，正是生成式人工智能  

**34.** Last year, some friends and I wrote an article called 'In Praise of Empathic AI.'  
> 去年，几位朋友与我共同撰写了一篇题为《赞美共情型人工智能》的文章  

**35.** If that title makes you cringe and makes you kind of angry, you're not alone.  
> 倘若这个标题令你本能反感甚至略感愤怒，那你绝非孤例  

**36.** Our colleagues hated it.  
> 我们的同事都很讨厌它。  

**37.** It's the article that I've written that most people hated.  
> 这是我写的文章，而大多数人对它深恶痛绝。  

**38.** But bear with me.  
> 但请耐心听我讲完。  

**39.** We started by making a sort of simple point, which is to review a set of studies that have involved — like, you get a whole lot of people interacting with other people, sometimes doctors, sometimes therapists, sometimes just regular people.  
> 我们首先提出了一个看似简单的观点，  

**40.** And you get a whole lot of people interacting with chatbots.  
> 同时，也让大量受试者与聊天机器人互动。  

**41.** And then you ask them later: “How did it go?  
> 随后，再向他们提问：‘过程如何？您的体验怎样？’  

**42.** What’s your experience?”  
> 随后，再向他们提问：‘过程如何？您的体验怎样？’  

**43.** And people tend to rate the chatbots as more empathic, more caring, more understanding.  
> 结果人们普遍评价聊天机器人更具共情力、更富关怀、更善理解。  

**44.** And it's no surprise.  
> 这并不令人意外。  

**45.** AIs are built to be attentive, to be present, to be interested.  
> 人工智能被设计为专注、在场且富有兴趣。  

**46.** An AI is never going to impatiently wait for you to finish telling your story, so it can tell you one of its own.  
> 人工智能绝不会不耐烦地等着你讲完自己的故事，  

**47.** (Laughter) An AI is never going to say, 'Dude, I'm busy.  
> 只为随即向你讲述它自己的一个故事。  

**48.** Get back to me later.  
> 稍后再联系我。  

**49.** I got things going on.'  
> 我手头正忙着呢。’  

**50.** Suppose some future — I don't think it's happening now, but some future AI could serve as a cure for loneliness.  
> 试想某种未来情形——我认为目前尚未发生，  

**51.** Would this be a good thing?  
> 这会是一件好事吗？  

**52.** Well to some extent, yes.  
> 某种程度上，的确如此。  

**53.** Obviously, yes.  
> 显然，答案是肯定的。  

**54.** Think about the many millions of elderly people, many in institutions, who go for days and nights without talking to anybody, in perfect agony of isolation.  
> 试想全球数以千万计的老年人，  

**55.** Many of them have dementia.  
> 他们中许多人患有痴呆症。  

**56.** Many of them are difficult to interact with for other reasons.  
> 出于其他原因，他们中的许多人难以与之交流。  

**57.** They don't have family and friends to spend time with them.  
> 他们没有家人和朋友陪伴左右。  

**58.** And there's not enough money in the world to pay people to come and talk to them for hours on end.  
> 世界上根本没有足够的资金，来雇人  

**59.** If some future version of ChatGPT or Claude or Gemini could ease these people's suffering, it would be a godsend.  
> 或Claude、或Gemini，能够缓解这些人的痛苦，  

**60.** But for the rest of us, it's more complicated.  
> 但对其他人而言，情况则更为复杂。  

**61.** And you can view these cures for loneliness as akin to a powerful painkiller.  
> 我们可以将这些治疗孤独的方案，视作一种强效止痛药。  

**62.** Like a powerful painkiller, it can alleviate suffering.  
> 正如强效止痛药能缓解病痛一样，它也能减轻痛苦。  

**63.** But like a powerful painkiller, it can sometimes come at a terrible cost.  
> 但正如强效止痛药一样，它有时会带来惨重代价。  

**64.** And to make sense of the cost, you have to think about what loneliness is for.  
> 要理解这一代价，我们必须思考孤独存在的意义。  

**65.** So the neuroscientist John Cacioppo, who founded the modern science of loneliness, argued that it's a biological signal akin to hunger, thirst and pain.  
> 他认为，孤独是一种生物学信号，类似于饥饿，  

**66.** And as such, it does two things for us.  
> 正因如此，它在两个方面作用于我们。  

**67.** It could sometimes be paralyzing, but when it works out well, it has certain evolved functions that improve our lives.  
> 它有时令人麻痹无力，  

**68.** So one, just as hunger motivates us to seek out food, loneliness and other forms of social suffering motivate us to seek out and maintain social connection.  
> 孤独及其他形式的社会性痛苦，驱使我们主动寻求，  

**69.** Because we get lonely, we accept an invitation to a party, we invite a friend out to brunch, we open up the dating apps.  
> 我们才会接受一场聚会的邀约，  

**70.** It also helps us regulate relationships.  
> 它还有助于我们调节人际关系。  

**71.** It helps us try to repair a relationship that has gone wrong, to apologize for messing up, and to try to show genuine interest in our relationship with someone else.  
> 并努力表现出对我们与他人之间关系的真诚关注。  

**72.** A second thing that loneliness does is help us grow as people.  
> 孤独的第二个作用是帮助我们作为个体成长。  

**73.** It's a truism in psychology that we learn from failure.  
> 在心理学中，我们从失败中学习是一个公认的事实。  

**74.** They call it trial-and-error learning—or reinforcement learning.  
> 他们称之为试错学习——或称强化学习。  

**75.** The idea is nicely summed up in the movie 'Batman Begins', where young Bruce Wayne’s father asks the future Batman, 'Why do we fall?'  
> 这一观点在电影《蝙蝠侠：侠影之谜》中得到了精妙的概括。  

**76.** And he answers, 'So we can learn to pick ourselves up.'  
> 他回答道：‘这样我们才能学会自己站起来。’  

**77.** This is literally true for toddlers who learn to walk by falling and stumbling and doing things wrong.  
> 这对学步幼儿而言确是如此——他们正是通过一次次跌倒来学习走路。  

**78.** It's true for a comedian who learns to improve her act by bombing on stage, by having her jokes fall flat.  
> 这对喜剧演员同样成立——她通过在舞台上冷场来改进自己的表演。  

**79.** It's true for a boxer who learns to evade a punch by getting repeatedly smacked in the face.  
> 这对拳击手亦然——他通过屡次被迎面击打来学会如何躲避拳头。  

**80.** Well, failure in the social realm is associated with loneliness.  
> 事实上，社交领域的失败往往与孤独感相伴而生。  

**81.** And can you imagine what it would be like if people never got the signal of failure?  
> 倘若人们从未接收到失败的信号？  

**82.** Imagine a teenage boy who never learned to cut short his monologues because his AI companion never got bored.  
> 试想一位从未学会适时结束独白的青少年男孩，  

**83.** (Laughter) Imagine an adult who never learned to apologize because her AI companion never needed an apology.  
> （笑声）再试想一位从未学会道歉的成年人，

**85.** (Laughter and Applause) Now you might think this has an easy technological fix.

> 你只需进入设置，将其调整得不那么阿谀奉承。  

**88.** And in fact, I don't use AI myself for social purposes, but I use it a lot for my work.  
> 我自己并不将人工智能用于社交目的，  

**89.** And after a while I got tired of Chat.

> 一个月后，我又改了回去。  

**100.** In Shakespeare's 'King Lear,' the king brings together his three daughters to decide how to divide the kingdom.  
> 在莎士比亚的《李尔王》中，国王召集三位女儿，以决定如何分割王国。

> 他要求每位女儿坦言自己对他的爱有多深。  

**102.** The first two daughters lavish praise on him.

> 我思忖着：在一切可能的世界中，最理想的一种，或许便是创造出不完美的伙伴，

> 这些伙伴足够难相处，以至于我们在与之互动的过程中得以成长。  

**135.** Companions that are sufficiently imperfect that we ultimately abandon them to deal with people.  
> 这些伙伴的不完美程度恰到好处，终将促使我们舍弃它们，转而直面真实的人际交往。  

**136.** Now I'm pretty confident we could build such things.  
> 如今，我相当确信，我们完全有能力造出这样的存在。  

**137.** I'm less confident that there's money to be had by doing so.
